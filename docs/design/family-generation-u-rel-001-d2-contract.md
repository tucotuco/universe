**PROPOSED — NOT IMPLEMENTED.** The parent-set semantics in §1 are user-confirmed design constraints. Ontology/storage, species mechanics, time rules, completeness machinery and publication remain proposals. No production source, tests, rules or legacy files were changed. No legacy code was executed or imported, and no legacy data was copied into the runtime.

## 1. User-confirmed parent-set semantics

These semantics are binding for this design, but do not by themselves authorize implementation:

1. A FamilyGroup comprises one **exact unordered set of reproductive parent identities** and all offspring of that set. Every offspring in that FamilyGroup has the same exact parent set; those offspring are full siblings under this reproductive definition. An offspring sharing only a subset of those parents belongs to a different FamilyGroup.
2. Half-sibling relationships are derived across distinct FamilyGroups by comparing parent sets. A Being can be offspring in one FamilyGroup and a parent in other FamilyGroups. Completing one FamilyGroup does not complete all offspring of any one parent across other groups.
3. Parent identity and reproductive role are independent of gender. Do not require male/female, a gender combination, exactly two parents, or permanent mother/father fields. A one-parent asexual family uses a singleton parent set. One-gender species and same-gender parent sets are valid. A hermaphroditic Being remains one identity even if it performs different reproductive roles at separate events; role changes do not change the exact parent set.
4. For a multi-contributor parent set, every contributor is an element of the same unordered identity set. Event-level roles/mechanisms may differ between offspring but cannot silently split a group whose exact set remains the same. If a new reproduction event has a different contributor set, it is a different FamilyGroup under these semantics.
5. Reproductive family membership is distinct from adoption/fostering, household, partnership, care, population membership and observation. Those may be represented independently; they do not alter the reproductive parent-set key unless an approved rule explicitly says so.

## 3. Ontology and boundaries

**Recommendation: design FamilyGroup as a first-class world-level reproductive structure, not a `StatisticalGroup` subtype and not necessarily an `ObjectInstance`.** It should own the scope, constraint inputs, parent/offspring links, revision, provenance and explicit completeness claims for one reproductive-family unit. The implementation representation could be a typed immutable record in a Universe-owned family registry/Reality service. That registry, durable identity namespace, and atomic owner are design dependencies, not existing APIs. A stable FamilyGroup ID must be distinct from all Being and population-member IDs.

| Candidate | Benefit | Problem / boundary | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. First-class FamilyGroup world entity / generative structure** | Gives a natural place for family-scoped constraints, unresolved state, progressive resolution and scoped completeness. Can exist without any Being or Encounter. Cross-family links can reference the same Person identity. | Requires new typed storage, identity and world transaction contracts. Must not inherit ObjectInstance’s physical-object semantics by convenience. | **Preferred design candidate, unadopted.** A domain entity/value in a Universe-owned family registry is the least misleading first representation. |
| **B. FamilyGroup as StatisticalGroup specialization** | Could reuse some keyed sampling and Reality utilities. | StatisticalGroup means aggregate population composition/households and has physical motion/footprint, member slots and current membership. FamilyGroup concerns parentage, not population count or residence; a person can span family structures and Groups. Inheritance would make those distinctions easy to violate. | Not preferred initially. Reuse narrowly reviewed deterministic/ledger ideas, not the subtype. |
| **C. Relation-graph-only design** | A shared graph can represent cross-family parentage and derive kinship without an entity hierarchy. | A bare graph has no clear family-specific distribution/constraint boundary, unresolved state, request scope, or completeness marker. It may still be the internal edge store beneath FamilyGroup. | Useful storage primitive, insufficient as the whole authoring/resolution API. |

### Distinct concepts

- **FamilyGroup:** one explicitly scoped reproductive configuration and its parent/offspring constraints. It is not a household, residence, social unit, population cohort, or generic relation bundle.
- **StatisticalGroup / population:** aggregate, potentially counted membership. Family membership does not add a member to a StatisticalGroup. A person can be represented by one stable identity in several family structures and may be outside, or associated with one or more population groups according to their distinct rules; no FamilyGroup association alone changes a count.
- **Being:** fully instantiated individual state, using the same identity as a reserved latent person if/when instantiated. A family member need not yet be a Being and may be historical/deceased or outside the current population.
- **Household, care, adoption, partnership:** separate relation families and scope. They must not be implied by reproductive parentage.
- **Events:** birth, death, migration, conception, or other time-bearing occurrences may eventually be typed world facts; Events remain ontologically distinct from Objects.

## 4. Reproductive parent-set invariant, roles, time and derivation

The exact parent set is a **confirmed design invariant**: one FamilyGroup has one exact, unordered set of stable reproductive-parent identities and contains all offspring attributed to that same set. Every child in it has exactly that set. Equal sets identify the same reproductive grouping; a child whose exact set differs belongs to another FamilyGroup. Full siblings are children with equal parent sets in that FamilyGroup. Parent identity is stable across role changes. An event can assign different reproductive roles to the same Being across events without changing the set. A singleton set is valid for asexual reproduction; any larger cardinality supported by a species is possible. Repeating one identity for two event roles still means one parent-set member, not two Beings.

Required worked checks: `{P,Q}` with C1/C2 is one FamilyGroup and they are full siblings independent of gender or role changes; `{P,R}` with C3 is another FamilyGroup and C3 is a half-sibling of C1/C2 through P in this two-parent case; asexual `{P}` with C4/C5 is valid; same-gender contributors in a one-gender species are accepted; hermaphroditic P may perform roles X and Y across events without splitting `{P,Q}` while the identity set is unchanged.

The following are not confirmed mechanics and require species/schema decisions:

- Which event facts identify an offspring as belonging to that parent set: conception, birth/hatching/emergence, another species-defined origin event, or an authored parentage assertion.
- Which per-event contributor roles to store. Genetic/progenitor contribution, gestation, birth, and other species mechanisms may be distinct. Role labels are event-scoped; do not infer them from gender or treat them as stable Being fields.
- Whether an offspring can have more than one natal/origin parent set, such as multiple independently sufficient reproductive events. The basic examples assume one relevant natal parent set per offspring; multi-origin cases need an explicit rule and cannot silently put one child into incompatible exact-set families.
- The exact “half-sibling” classification for parent sets larger than two. The confirmed derivation compares exact sets across FamilyGroups; a shared proper subset makes a cross-group sibling relationship, while the label/degree for complex contributor sets needs species/schema policy.

Objective primitive facts should include exact stable parent identity set, offspring identity, FamilyGroup identity, origin/reproduction-event reference, the approved event-specific role map, relevant times/intervals, and provenance. Derive full siblings from equal exact sets; derive half-sibling/partial-parent connections across different FamilyGroups by comparing their sets (for the confirmed P/Q versus P/R example, the shared P establishes the relation). Derive ancestors from the approved parentage graph. Adoption, foster, care, partnership and household relations are distinct facts and must never change the reproductive parent-set key.

Temporal, biological and cycle rules beyond the exact set semantics are proposals only. A species policy may require an origin event after a parent's existence/start time, constrain event contributor roles, or prohibit an ancestry cycle. Do not invent a universal gestation, age threshold, fertility rule, gender relation or timeline. Current Beings have no age/birth-date field. Unknown event time must remain explicitly unresolved; it cannot silently satisfy a time-bounded completeness claim. Deceased or migrated offspring remain in historical family membership; their current population status is separate. Author-authored parent sets and sampled parent sets need distinct provenance.

## 5. Progressive elaboration and completeness

The proposed model separates these operations; each can be committed independently only with a suitable revision and dependency contract:

1. **Create unresolved FamilyGroup:** allocate a stable family ID; store authored constraints/distribution references and provenance. The exact parent identity set may be unresolved; offspring count, identities, events, attributes and Being instances may also remain unresolved. Creation can happen with no Encounter and no Observation. A parent-set index is not available until every parent identity is resolved.
2. **Resolve the exact parent set:** resolve or reserve a complete unordered set of stable parent identities. It is not valid to partially treat a set as exact. Once complete, canonical set equality determines whether a matching FamilyGroup already exists. Event-specific roles do not add duplicate parent identities.
3. **Resolve offspring count/identities:** commit an explicit count or childless result for a bounded scope, then reserve stable offspring person identities. An unresolved count differs from a complete zero-offspring claim. Reserving a latent identity does not construct a Being or add a population member.
4. **Establish reproduction events and chronology:** bind each offspring to that FamilyGroup's exact parent set and event-specific role map; add approved event/effective times after joint validation against Reality and other groups.
5. **Resolve individual attributes:** fill explicitly requested traits such as age/date, physiology or history under approved distributions and constraints. Family completeness does not imply attribute completeness.
6. **Instantiate a Being:** materialize an already reserved identity through its own native constructor and publication boundary. The identity must not change; Being state can remain partial only where native model permits. It is distinct from family membership and population membership.

### Completeness claims

“Complete” must always be scoped. A claim should name the FamilyGroup or global query, relation semantics and roles covered, horizon (including its inclusive/exclusive boundary), member-identity status, parentage status, and schema/revision. Independent dimensions should say `unresolved`, `partial`, or `complete` for parent roles, child identities, parentage, chronology, selected attributes, and Being instantiation.

Example: “FamilyGroup FG with exact parent set `{P,Q}` has all offspring events through `t` committed, with each offspring identity and exact `{P,Q}` membership resolved; attributes and Being instances may remain unresolved.” This must not mean “P has no other children in another FamilyGroup,” “P/Q have no children after `t`,” or “all offspring of P globally are known.” A later `{P,Q}` child with an event time `<=t` contradicts the scoped closure and must be refused or require an explicit, versioned correction/supersession; a `{P,Q}` child after `t` or a child of `{P,R}` is not a contradiction to this claim.

- **Zero children:** a resolved parent set `{P}` or `{P,Q}` may have zero offspring through a bounded horizon; record that explicit zero plus the exact completeness claim. Unknown parent set/count has no zero claim. Later offspring may be added outside the closed horizon.
- **Deceased or migrated children:** identity and historical parentage remain in Reality. Death/migration is separate typed time history. A deceased or moved child is not silently removed from the FamilyGroup’s historical scope.
- **Population overlap:** a FamilyGroup does not change any StatisticalGroup count. If a generated person belongs to an established counted population, bind that person identity to one already-counted slot or run a separate explicitly authorized population transaction. Never count the same Being twice because it participates in several FamilyGroups.
- **Unresolved details:** family role completeness may be true while individual attributes, life events, observation, or Being instantiation remain unresolved.

## 6. Cross-FamilyGroup parentage

Let `FG-PQ` have exact parent set `{P,Q}` and children `C1,C2`. Let `FG-PR` have exact set `{P,R}` and child `C3`. `C1` and `C2` are full siblings under the confirmed exact-set rule. `C3` is a half-sibling across groups through shared parent P in the two-parent case. P can be offspring in `FG-X` while being a parent in both `FG-PQ` and `FG-PR`; those references use the same stable Person/Being identity. A one-parent group `{P}` may coexist with other parent sets involving P. A hermaphroditic P can perform role X for one event and Y for another while remaining one member of each set containing P; event-role changes alone never split or merge sets.

No FamilyGroup owns or duplicates a Being. The same Person ID can participate in several family structures and in zero or one statistical population slot according to population rules. A global “all offspring of P through t” query must union all distinct exact parent sets containing P and include the offspring claims for each relevant FamilyGroup. It requires a global resolver/index and a global completeness claim. Completion of `FG-PQ` cannot assert P has no offspring in `FG-PR`, `{P}`, or future groups. Deduplication is by Person identity and the approved origin-event semantics, not by family count or population slot count.

## 7. World request, detached candidate and result

The following is a candidate data contract, not an API declaration:

- **`FamilyGroupSpec` (data only):** stable FamilyGroup ID; schema/version; exact unordered parent identity set or explicit unresolved-parent-set state; relation semantics; time domain; constraints; optional validated distribution IDs/versions; provenance; and requested completeness scope. An unresolved parent set cannot be compared as complete or used to close offspring. It contains no executable generator.
- **Parent-set identity:** compare canonicalized complete identity sets, independent of input order or event roles. A hash can index the sorted set but must be checked against the full set to detect collisions. Consider a distinct stable FamilyGroup ID allocated before parent resolution, plus a unique index from resolved exact parent-set key to that ID. Once the exact set is known, another FamilyGroup with the same exact set reuses the existing group or is refused as a duplicate; it must not split same-set offspring across groups. Which IDs are assigned to unresolved latent parents, and whether the parent-set key itself is the family ID, remain engineering decisions. The recommended separate FamilyGroup ID avoids changing group identity when an unresolved set is completed.
- **`FamilyResolutionRequest` (frozen):** trusted world-authority origin; idempotency key; operation (create unresolved group, resolve exact parent set, resolve offspring, establish event/relations, attributes, or bounded all-offspring query); target FamilyGroup or global parent anchor; requested resolution/completeness level; inclusive/exclusive horizon; expected Reality/family/Group revisions; deterministic seed/draw-owner reference where approved; and provenance. It has no Agent recipient or Observation argument.
- **`FamilyResolutionCandidate` (detached immutable):** normalized request; exact read/dependency set including parent-set index and all scoped family/population sources; logical identity reservations; exact parent sets; all proposed objective offspring/event/time/population facts; explicit local/global completeness claims; deterministic provisional draw trace/state; coverage certificate; and exact write/rollback/alias plan. Preparation neither calls mutating `StatisticalGroup.member()` nor allocates authoritative IDs, mutates caches, consumes authoritative RNG, changes counts, changes Beings, or touches Belief/Awareness.
- **`FamilyResolutionResult` (data only):** commit revision, stable FamilyGroup/person identities, committed facts/provenance, resolution level and exact completeness claims. It is a world-authoring result; it contains no evidence, Awareness or Belief update.

### Identity and population closure

Reserve logical Person IDs in a world-level namespace independent of names, materialization, Group membership, or Being object allocation. A FamilyGroup ID is not a parent identity. The exact parent set is unordered and stable: canonical ordering is for comparison/encoding only, never a reproductive role order. Existing Being/Group slot IDs may be referenced only when ownership/read dependencies prove membership. A reserved identity must be reusable by later FamilyGroups and Being materialization; collision checks span the Universe ObjectRegistry, unresolved/resolved Person reservations, Group slots, histories, and Encounter participants where present. Do not expose unresolved identity reservations to Agents. The namespace, stable FamilyGroup ID, parent-set key, and deterministic derivation for latent parents/children need approval.

For a counted Group, candidate closure must prove every referenced slot exists in its committed category/household count; preserve the entire category-count map and totals, not just total humans. New offspring not already represented by a counted slot may exist as historical/out-of-Group logical people, but must not be injected into a Group count implicitly. If the intended result changes population counts, that requires a separate authored population write and transaction.

### Determinism, requests and refusal

Candidate construction is pure. Authored complete family data needs no random draw. If later approved distribution sampling is used, derive provisional draws from stable world seed + FamilyGroup/member/fact namespace and the canonical generation scope, with recorded algorithm/table versions. Commit RNG advancement only with successful publication. Do not use observer RNG or an ambient module-global stream.

**Replay** requires the same initial state, schema, ordered requests and draw owner to reproduce the same results. **Request-order independence** requires independent facts for the same anchors to be invariant to unrelated request order. These are different guarantees. Per-member keyed streams help independent resolution; a family-wide child count, shared constraints or coupled sibling dates may still depend on joint canonical resolution. The initial B gate should be authored and nonrandom; no RNG guarantee should be claimed beyond purity.

Reject stale dependencies, duplicate or colliding reservations, incomplete candidate coverage, incompatible time/roles, contradiction with committed Reality, and unsatisfiable constraint sets before writes. Do not reroll to conceal a contradiction, select a different parent silently, shift a date, or overwrite a prior fact. Same idempotency key and identical normalized request returns the original result without additional facts/revision; same key with different content refuses. Any publication failure restores all world/family/population writes and RNG in place; observer ledgers remain unchanged. The current Encounter publisher does not implement this no-Encounter world transaction.

## 8. Worked pseudodata examples (not executable code)

### FG0 — observation-free FamilyGroup creation and full authored elaboration

At world time `t0`, an author creates `FG0` directly in the world family registry. No Encounter exists and no Agent or Observation is supplied.

**Unresolved form:** `FG0` has a stable FamilyGroup ID, authored role semantics version, time domain and source provenance; parent identities and offspring count are unresolved; no person slots, relation facts or completeness claims have been committed. The record says explicitly that it is unresolved, not childless.

**Fully elaborated authored form:** the author supplies exact unordered parent set `{P,Q}`, children `{C1,C2}`, event facts with any adopted times/roles, and an offspring/parentage completeness claim for this exact set through `t0`. C1 and C2 share precisely `{P,Q}` and are full siblings even if P/Q have the same gender or different roles across events. P can independently belong to another group such as `{P,R}`. Person attributes may remain unresolved, and none needs to be instantiated as a Being for the family facts to be objective. A separate no-Encounter world transaction validates all references and commits the facts together. Agent evidence, Awareness, Belief and observer histories remain unchanged.

The corresponding asexual form is a distinct FamilyGroup with parent set `{A}` and children `{A1,A2}`; it has one parent identity, not a duplicated identity for multiple event roles. A same-gender or one-gender contributor set is accepted by identity semantics. If hermaphroditic P uses role X for one event and role Y for another while the contributor identity set remains `{P,Q}`, both offspring remain in that same FamilyGroup.

### B — one relation between two already-counted slots in the 11-human Group

World-authorized pseudoreferences `P-slot` and `C-slot` name an already-counted parent-eligible and child-eligible slot in the 11-human StatisticalGroup after actual committed category counts are checked. Candidate proposes one authored relation edge and provenance. It creates no Being and no population entry. Before/after every category, human and household count is identical. No Observation/Belief changes. If category/temporal eligibility is not established, refuse; do not infer it from the total 11. This single edge does **not** identify the complete parent set, establish that this is a singleton-parent family, create/complete a FamilyGroup, enumerate siblings, or validate same-set membership. It is only an initial validator test fixture and must not become the permanent public family-generation API.

### A — all offspring of one Being through time `t`

Let `FG-PQ` hold parent set `{P,Q}` and children `{C1,C2}`; let `FG-PR` hold `{P,R}` and child `{C3}`. Query `Q-A` asks for all offspring of P through `t`. It traverses both FamilyGroups (and every other family source in its declared completeness domain), includes earlier committed children, deduplicates Person IDs, and reports C1/C2 as full siblings and C3 as a cross-group half-sibling with C1/C2 through P in this two-parent example. A deceased child and a nonresident child still appear in historical parentage; neither is silently inserted into the current 11-human Group. The global result may claim P's offspring across all applicable exact parent sets are complete through `t`; `FG-PQ` alone cannot make that claim. It does not close the interval after `t`, nor another unresolved origin source. No Agent learns the result automatically.
