## Rules and architecture — inspect before implementation

**Authority:** `docs/encounters/README.md`, adopted revision-61 working draft **plus all later adopted supplements through r69**, especially Stun, Stun skill, Subdue, Subdual, Unarmed Combat, Attack Roll / critical / fatal, hit-point thresholds, Fortitude saves, Combat Strategy, armor/shields, action interruption, common-snapshot second resolution, and recovery. Use the *latest adopted* text, not original superseded RTF wording, old examples where editorially incomplete, or prior test assumptions. Review existing `src/` Action/Choice/Being condition and checkpoint contracts and generated-Arena handles; prefer native extension over cloned evaluators or browser-side HP changes.

**Stun — adopted distinctions to preserve:**

- Stun is a **declared special unarmed strike**, requiring acquired extraordinary progressive **Stun** skill. It uses ordinary Unarmed Combat timing and attack rules, subject to the Stun skill's creature-category-specific advanced threat-range benefit and armor check penalty. Do not infer advanced category training or immunity when source facts are missing; support explicitly represented evidence.
- An adjusted attack DC greater than 20 or an actually critical-immune target **cannot be stunned**, even if the underlying attack hits. A miss deals no damage. A hit that is not a threat, or an eligible threat whose Fortitude DC-20 save succeeds, is **failed Stun** and inflicts **half normal damage, rounded down**. The same reduced-damage rule applies to other Stun failure causes even if the critical threat is confirmed.
- For an eligible threat, failed Fortitude DC 20 **stuns regardless of critical confirmation**. Successful Stun duration is the sum of **one d6 per Stun skill level** in seconds. On successful Stun, confirmed critical applies normal critical damage, otherwise normal damage. The adopted **two-natural-20 fatal-hit outcome remains operative** even if Stun fails; do not create a fatal immunity by selecting Stun. Apply ordinary protection, injury and saving-throw rules only at their lawful boundary.
- Newly committed Stun interrupts an Action still in progress whose completion is in a **later** second; an Action resolving in the **same** second remains eligible and resolves from the same pre-action snapshot. Effect duration, incapacitation, repeated attempts, overlapping causes and expiration must be represented without retroactive cancellation or manual per-second bookkeeping.

**Subdue — distinct injury/condition channels:**

- Subdue is a **declared melee attack** using an allowed **bludgeon** penetration type, with the applicable normal weapon timing and attack/protection prerequisites. **Ranged subdual is prohibited.** An unsupported penetration type or target known immune to subdual must fail lawfully, not silently convert to ordinary Strike.
- Track **subdual damage separately from normal HP damage**. For every successful Subdue attack, apply the adopted subdual damage and **half of the damage (rounded down) as ordinary damage** as specified by the rule; do not conflate the two counters or convert Subdue into a lethal-only ordinary attack. Subdual damage sufficient to meet or exceed the target's remaining HP makes the target unconscious under the adopted condition model. Ordinary damage can still produce negative HP and the usual injury/death rules.
- Subdual-induced unconsciousness persists **independently** of injury-related, mental-fatigue or other incapacity. Healing ordinary HP to 1 does not automatically erase subdual unconsciousness. Subdual damage is removed **as fatigue at a base rate of one point per hour**; implement authoritative time-based recovery/checkpoint continuation without hand-maintained fatigue ticks or invented accelerated recovery. Do not assume that a Being at exactly 0 HP is conscious.
- For simultaneous independent Subdue strikes, read relevant original target facts from the same postmovement/pre-action snapshot, stage compatible effects, and commit them together. No earlier-processed hit can remove a same-second opponent's legal Action. Preserve existing fatal and ordinary-damage stacking / rollback semantics; where threshold interaction is genuinely unspecified, document the exact unresolved case rather than inventing order or per-roll mutations.
