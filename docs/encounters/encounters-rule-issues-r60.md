## A. Contradictions and identifiable defects inside the rules draft

### ES-001 — Unarmed strategy pool described as doubled twice
- **Status:** Resolved — adopted in revision 2 · **Priority:** Critical · **Source:** [p. 15](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#pdf-page-15), [p. 17](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#pdf-page-17).
- **Evidence:** Combat Strategy says to use “double your Unarmed combat skill level twice (the second one represents your body as a weapon),” suggesting four times the level. The Unarmed Combat section says **twice**, and the worked example assigns 10 points to a character with Unarmed combat level 5.
- **Editorial r1 (now adopted):** Wording corrected to **twice the Unarmed combat skill level**, consistent with p. 17. Does not add extra attribute points; unarmed worked-example test still needed. **Adopted 21 October 2026.**
- **Proposed decision:** State explicitly that the base unarmed pool is `2 × Unarmed combat level`, plus applicable modifiers, or consciously adopt another formula and update the example.
- **Acceptance test:** Level-5 unarmed fighter, no modifiers, has exactly the agreed number of strategy points.

### ES-002 — Passive shield interception: d10 versus d6

- **Adopted r2:** The [Shields rule](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#shields) on p. 28 is authoritative; Strike step 2 now refers to it rather than using the conflicting d6, hardness, or randomized absorption procedure. Strike step 3 now distinguishes damage stopped from shield HP loss. **Adopted 21 October 2026.**
- **Acceptance test:** Exhaustively enumerate all die faces and verify interception probabilities for shield sizes 1–5.

### ES-003 — Passive shield absorption and shield damage have different formulas

## B. Rules that need precise interpretation before implementation

### ES-010 — Fatal-hit Fortitude saving throw DC
- **Status:** Resolved — adopted in revision 4 · **Source:** [Threat/fatal hit, p. 12](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#pdf-page-12), [Saving throws, p. 14](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#pdf-page-14).
- **Resolution:** Fatal-hit avoidance is a Fortitude **saving throw**, not a Constitution ability check. The default **DC 20** applies unless an explicit exception is given. There is no need to derive its DC from the attack roll.
- **Acceptance test:** A fatal-hit double-natural-20 requests a Fortitude d20 save with default DC 20, Constitution-related modifiers, natural-1 failure and natural-20 success.

### ES-011 — Rule timing and action decision boundary
- **Status:** Resolved — adopted in revision 8 · **Priority:** High · **Source:** [Encounter cycle and rounds, pp. 8–9](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#one-second-action-and-defense-resolution).
- **Adopted:** An action declared at the end of second `t` with duration `d` resolves during second `t+d`. The choice of another combatant's action is not perceptible in the same second as its declaration; detection can begin at the start of the next second if circumstances permit. Actions not yet completed may be aborted in accordance with the existing rules. Decisions to apply prepared defenses must occur before their selected incoming attack is resolved.
- **Acceptance test:** A three-second attack chosen at the end of second 0 resolves in second 3; no agent can react to another's newly declared choice in the same second without an independent source of information.

### ES-012 — Simultaneity must govern all consequences
- **Status:** Resolved — adopted in revision 8 and generalized explicitly in revision 10 · **Priority:** High · **Source:** [Rounds, pp. 8–9](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#one-second-action-and-defense-resolution).
- **Adopted:** After the movement phase, all non-movement actions completing in the same second are evaluated against the same pre-action Being/Object snapshot; **no** Being or Object state is updated until all these actions resolve. Results commit simultaneously. Neither action's outcome can retroactively alter another action in that second. Selection of which attacks receive limited protection must be committed before knowing outcomes. Tests for shared-resource conflicts remain implementation work, not a license to change the universal state-commitment principle.
- **Acceptance test:** Simultaneous lethal strikes both complete; breaking a weapon in a simultaneous action does not prevent the weapon's already scheduled strike.

### ES-013 — Prepared Parry, Dodge, and reaction timing
- **Status:** Resolved at principle level — adopted in revision 8 · **Priority:** High · **Source:** [Defense actions, p. 60](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#one-second-action-and-defense-resolution).
- **Adopted:** Parry/Dodge can begin without a target and become ready after their respective preparation time. Parry uses the defender's own weapon timing. A ready defense stays available until expended, aborted, or forfeited; it can be used in the second it becomes ready or any later second against a perceived eligible attack. The decision to use it is made before that attack's outcome; once used it must be newly prepared for a later attack. One ready defense does not by itself cover multiple simultaneous attacks. Existing skill, weight, and Multiple Actions constraints remain in force.
- **Acceptance test:** A four-second Parry begun at the end of second 0 can be held until second 6 and either committed to a known strike before rolling or retained; a spent Parry is not available for a second strike without preparation.

### ES-014 — Strategy points, negative modifiers and category-specific caps
- **Status:** **Resolved — adopted in revision 9** · **Priority:** High · **Source:** [Combat Strategy, pp. 15–16](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#combat-strategy), [Strategy-point allocation limits](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#strategy-point-allocation-limits).
- **Adopted:** The available pool is the sum of applicable action-specific contributions with a floor of **zero**. An otherwise legal action is still possible with zero strategy points. Allocating fewer points than are available is legal; unspent points confer no benefit and cannot be reassigned while the action remains in progress. **Exceeding any absolute total or category limit must be rejected immediately** at declaration; there is no automatic clamping, discarding, or redistribution. A negative calculated category cap is treated as **zero**. Existing timing costs, action-specific formulas, and prerequisites are unchanged.
- **Acceptance tests:** For base result -3, pool=0. From pool 5, allocation 2 attack + 1 defense and 2 unspent is legal; it remains locked after action initiation. An extra-damage allocation of 3 against cap 1 is rejected without committing an action. Extra-damage or timing capacity -1 results in maximum 0; requests for any positive amount are rejected.

### ES-015 — Critical hits, shields, armor, and universal state commitment
- **Status:** **Resolved — adopted in revision 10** · **Priority:** Medium · **Source:** [Critical/fatal hits, p. 12](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#threat-critical-hit-fatal-hit), [Shields, p. 28](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#shields), [Strike, p. 57](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#strike-swing-or-thrust), and [one-second action resolution](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#one-second-action-and-defense-resolution).
- **Adopted:** All actions resolving in the same second use one common post-movement, pre-action snapshot of **all** Being and Object states. All actions are evaluated before any Being or Object state changes are committed. Shield and armor protection applies using the pre-action equipment state to all covered attacks in that second, even if the combined resulting damage destroys the equipment. Its new state takes effect for subsequent seconds. This is an **universal rule**, not shield-specific. A critical hit's first (ordinary) weapon-damage component follows normal protection; its second unmodified weapon-damage roll bypasses shield and armor protection and does not cause shield/armor durability loss. Shield-size limits on the number of simultaneously protected attacks remain in force and assignment occurs before outcomes are known.
- **Acceptance tests:** (1) Shield size 3, HP 2, ordinary component 8, critical component 4, successful interception: stop 3 ordinary damage, send 5 ordinary to armor, apply 4 unprotected critical damage, and apply shield HP loss 5 only in final batch commit. (2) Two assigned same-second attacks both receive the shield's pre-action protection even if combined durability damage destroys it; subsequent-second attack receives none. (3) Analogous armor destruction preserves protection for all same-second actions. (4) Simultaneous death, disarm/breakage, and adverse condition changes do not alter success/eligibility of other actions resolving in the same second.

### ES-016 — Reach, attack clearance, and directional engagement
- **Status:** **Resolved at current single-hex scope — adopted in revisions 11–12** · **Priority:** Medium · **Source:** [Reach and Engagement, p. 13](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#engagement), [Attack Motions, p. 12](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#attack-motions).
- **Reach:** Ordinary single-hex Beings use center-to-center distances; weapon-specific reach is personal reach plus weapon length. Multi-hex Being geometry is deferred.
- **Attack clearance:** Entire attack trajectory, including weapon and necessary limb motion, must be unobstructed. Weapon- and motion-specific minimum effective distances require future Object statistics and are deferred, not invented.
- **Engagement:** Any opponent within the Being's forward reach is **engaged by** that Being, independent of attacking or readiness. Engagement is asymmetric and is **not a separate action**. The former three-foot reach-advantage special attack-roll test and persistent closing privilege are **deleted**.
- **Movement and attacks:** Relative movement is resolved before non-movement attacks; if the target is beyond attack reach afterward, the attack fails as out of reach. No withdrawal rule allows a successful out-of-reach hit.
- **Adjacent resolved:** ES-036 removes withdrawal-specific zero defense; ES-037 now resolves the armed example without longer-reach first-strike priority; ES-038 revises Charge separately. No separate engagement action is introduced.
- **Acceptance tests:** Directionality and asymmetric reach; changing engagement with movement or facing; no special 3-ft threshold; out-of-reach failure after movement; full path clearance; center-based single-hex targets.

### ES-017 — Numeric precision, rate rounding, and measurement units
- **Status:** **Resolved — adopted in revision 17** · **Priority:** Medium · **Source:** [Numerical precision and units](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#numerical-precision-and-units), [Movement](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#movement), [Natural healing](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#natural-healing), [Physical exertion](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#physical-exertion), [Throwing](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#throwing).
- **Adopted in r16:** Preserve full precision for intermediate quantities; respect explicit local rounding rules; record continuous spatial coordinates to **0.1 m** precision at movement commitment. For nonzero rate-based changes to whole-unit state, round calculated magnitude **up** after all modifiers and over the defined interval; physical exertion is an explicit **round-down exception**. Maximum thrown damage die size is rounded to **nearest integer** (half up). Preserve the original yard-dominant measurement thresholds, converting to meter coordinates numerically instead of relabeling.
- **Adopted in r17:** Calculate exertion across each uninterrupted activity interval and round down **once**; fractional remainders are **discarded permanently** when that interval ends, never carried to another activity. If formula-derived maximum thrown-object damage die size rounds to **zero**, the attack inflicts **zero damage** from that calculation, without rolling a die or forcing d1.
- **Acceptance tests:** 1 yard → 0.9144 m → committed coordinate 0.9 m. Healing 1.7 HP/day → 2 and 0.7 → 1; double 1.1 under rest then ceil = 3. Exertion 0.7 then another separate 0.7 interval → 0 total, while one uninterrupted 1.4 interval → 1. Thrown die maximum 0.49 → zero damage; 0.5 → d1; 1.6 → d2. Intermediate rounding is prohibited unless explicitly instructed.
- **Remaining nonblocking editorial backlog:** If desired, future conversion of all written source measurements into metric values, preserving distances and game outcomes exactly.

### ES-018 — Consciousness and stabilization at zero or negative HP
- **Status:** **Resolved — adopted in revision 17** · **Priority:** Medium · **Source:** [Hit Points and Damage](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#hit-points-and-damage).
- **Adopted in r16:** Injury at **0 HP or below** causes unconsciousness and prevents voluntary action. At **1 HP or more**, injury-caused unconsciousness ends, subject to independent conditions. Zero HP is stable; negative HP requires stabilization; -10 HP is death. No Fortitude roll restores consciousness at or below 0 HP. Healing from negative HP to zero establishes stability, not consciousness. Magical stabilization permits natural healing directly; subdual and mental fatigue are independent causes of unconsciousness.
- **Adopted in r17:** Eliminate the provisional hourly eligibility-to-recover check. Following stabilization at negative HP, make **one** Fortitude DC 20 recovery check after one hour; success permits natural healing, failure costs 1 HP and leads to **one daily recovery check** until success or death. The one-hour first opportunity retains the earliest timing of the older sequence, but there is **no two-check process**, no other hourly recovery roll, and no consciousness roll at 0 HP or below. **Stabilization checks every minute remain separate and unchanged.**
- **Acceptance tests:** A stabilized negative-HP Being has exactly one recovery roll after one hour and no eligibility roll; subsequent failures generate one daily check, not recurring hourly rolls. HP -1 → 0 produces stable/unconscious; HP 0 → 1 ends injury-related unconsciousness; external causes can still keep the Being unconscious. Resolve concurrent HP/state changes as a single batch.

### ES-019 — Combat skills and environmental qualifications
- **Status:** **Resolved at the defined scope — adopted in revision 18** · **Priority:** Medium · **Source:** [Combat Types and Environmental combat qualifications, p. 15](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#environmental-combat-qualifications), [Mounted Combat, pp. 23–24](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#mounted-combat), [Terrestrial/Aerial/Aquatic skills, pp. 47–50](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#pdf-page-47).
- **Adopted:** Choose the environmental qualification by the **attacker's environment of action**, not the target's location. Standing on the ground, on a ship's deck, or waist-deep in water is terrestrial; attacking while flying is aerial; swimming or attacking while submerged is aquatic. The skill applies to every combat type. Missing a required non-progressive qualification reduces the **effective relevant progressive combat-type skill** to zero, not the Being's underlying acquired skill, and does not negate distinct weapon/ability contributions. Where the weapon skill is itself the combat-type proficiency (bow skill for archery), it has no effective combat-type contribution in an environment for which the attacker lacks the required qualification; the same raw skill is not also added back as a distinct second proficiency. All simultaneously required non-combat qualifications must be present to use progressive combat training. For mounted combat, a combat-trained mount and Ride level >= 1 remain actual prerequisites, the Ride level still caps effective combat skill when qualified, and environmental qualification belongs to the rider, not the mount. Movement penalties, including wading penalties, are independent of skill qualification.
- **Acceptance tests:** (1) Land archer aiming at flying target needs the archer's Terrestrial, not Aerial, qualification. (2) Flying attacker striking a ground target needs Aerial. (3) Waist-deep but standing swordfighter uses Terrestrial; swimming/submerged swordfighter uses Aquatic; deck-bound sailor uses Terrestrial. (4) Armed Combat 5, longsword skill 3, and absent required Aquatic qualification while swimming -> effective Armed Combat 0, longsword skill remains 3. (5) Aerial-qualified pegasus rider with Armed Combat 5, Ride 2 -> effective combat-type skill 2; same rider lacking Aerial -> 0 even if the mount can fly. (6) A missing appropriate Ride level or untrained mount still disallows participating in mounted combat. (7) Bow skill serving as Archery combat proficiency contributes effective 0 without the required environment qualification; it is not counted again as a separate skill contribution. (8) Wading movement multipliers apply independent of qualification.

### ES-050 — Racial aptitude skill names and incompatible percentage semantics
- **Status:** **Resolved — refined in revision 25** · **Priority:** High · **Source:** [Racial Aptitude Table, p. 46](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#racial-aptitude-table), [Discovery actions](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#encounter-resolution), ES-050 revision 25 decision notes.
- **Adopted in r23–24:** Search, Spot, and Listen are **actions**, not trainable skills; aptitude percentages modify action success, not skill XP costs. Deliberate actions take **6 seconds**, cannot be shortened through strategy timing, and use d20 discovery DC checks. Spot applies at line-of-sight distance, Search only at accessible places in six seconds, and Listen where sounds are audible.
- **Adopted in r25:** Every discoverable Entity has **DC 15 by default** when no specific DC is defined, including Search. **+10% racial action aptitude = +2 d20**; **+5% = +1 d20**. **Elf**: Search/Spot/Listen +2. **Half-elf**: Search/Spot/Listen +1 (table changed from +10% to +5%). **Gnome**: Listen +2. **Halfling**: Listen +2, retaining the existing +10% row that was mischaracterized in r23–24 commentary.
- **Regression tests:** Entity without discovery DC uses 15 for all three actions; with DC 18 uses 18; relevant Spot/Hearing integer DC-table modifiers remain applicable. Half-elf d20+1 for all three, Elf d20+2 for all three, Gnome +2 Listen, Halfling +2 Listen. Aptitude changes do not affect skill learning or encounter-distance percentages.

### ES-051 — Timing of automatically triggered Spot and Listen checks
- **Status:** **Resolved — adopted in revision 25** · **Priority:** Medium · **Source:** [Encounter initial awareness](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#encounter-resolution), [Discovery actions](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#encounter-resolution), ES-051 decision notes.
- **Adopted:** **An environmental discovery opportunity, not an intentional action declaration, triggers incidental Spotting or Hearing checks** for Beings capable of perceiving the Entity or occurrence. These checks use the ordinary d20-versus-discovery-DC procedure, including racial aptitude and modifiers, **without consuming six-second action time, interrupting a continuing action, or requiring attention to be intentionally directed**. Failure can represent inattention. Initial encounter awareness is an instance of this procedure. This does not grant a zero-time voluntarily repeatable action. Failed checks do not self-trigger retries; an independent meaningful opportunity is required. Deliberate Spot and Listen actions still take six seconds without timing reduction.
- **Regression tests:** A Being engaged in another action may incidentally detect a newly audible sound without stopping that action; a failed incidental Spot check does not cause automatic one-second retry rolls. Deliberate Spot/Listen still consume six seconds. Entity discovery DC 15 default, situational modifiers, and racial bonuses apply equally to incidental and deliberate checks. Respect established same-second event visibility and commitment.

## C. Current Universe implementation differs from the written rules

These are **not** claims of errors in the draft. They are discrepancies in the current code that should be resolved using the draft or a documented amendment before calling the encounter rules-compliant.

### ES-020 — Weapon skill appears to be counted twice for accuracy
- **Status:** Implementation mismatch · **Priority:** Critical · **Source:** [Combat Strategy, p. 15](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#pdf-page-15), [armed worked example, p. 16](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#pdf-page-16).
- **Code:** `src/action.py:roll_hits` adds the actor’s weapon skill level **directly to the d20** and then adds the chosen attack strategy. The example uses combat+weapon skill+Dexterity to *form the strategy pool*, then applies the **allocated** attack points (3) to the attack roll—not a second unallocated weapon-skill bonus.
- **Consequence:** Accuracy is inflated compared with the worked example, changing tactics and hit frequency.
- **Remedy/test:** Introduce a single explicit strategy-budget function and make the attack roll depend on the allocated attack points and explicitly documented modifiers. Reproduce A’s need for a 13 to hit B in the p. 16 example.

### ES-021 — Strategy budget omits combat skill and ability modifiers
- **Status:** Implementation mismatch · **Priority:** Critical · **Source:** [pp. 15–17](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#pdf-page-15).
- **Code:** `src/tactical_duel.py:_effort_strategy` sets its budget to **weapon skill alone**, whereas the draft’s armed example uses Armed combat 2 + weapon skill 2 + Dex 1 = 5.
- **Consequence:** The human and AI share an internally consistent restriction, but it is not the published restriction; strategy options may be artificially narrow.
- **Remedy/test:** Derive the budget from action type, skills, attributes, fatigue, equipment and magic. Check the p. 16 and p. 17 examples.

### ES-022 — Extra damage is added as a fixed number instead of rolled

### ES-040 — Feint success test, effect, and duration
- **Status:** **Resolved — adopted in revision 20** · **Priority:** High · **Source:** [Feint, p. 53](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#feint), [Attack Roll, p. 11](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#attack-roll).
- **Adopted:** When the Feint resolves, determine success with the same Attack Roll criteria as any attack (normal d20, DC, strategy allocation and applicable defense/modifiers). A Feint is a deceptive false attack and does not deal weapon damage. On failure the combatant simply chooses a new action through the normal decision cycle, receiving no bonus. On success the Feinter's **next chosen action** must be an attack against **the same victim**: that follow-up attack gets **+2 to hit**. The benefit expires upon resolution of that attack whether it succeeds or fails. Choosing any other next action, including an attack against a different target, forfeits the benefit.
- **Acceptance tests:** Failed Feint → no benefit; successful Feint followed by an attack on the same target → +2 to that attack only; hit/miss both consume the bonus at the follow-up attack's resolution; next chosen action of a different type or against a different target → forfeiture. Respect ordinary one-second decision and action-resolution timing; no immediate extra action is granted.

### ES-041 — Grapple opposed-check formula and timing
- **Revision 54 adopted calibration principle (author):** The Being's maximum force capacity is intrinsic to the Being, based on Strength and physical characteristics rather than independently computed action by action. Calibrate it against the existing Strength-indexed **Carrying Capacity**, **Throwing**, and **Velocity versus Weight** tables. Heavy-load limits provide the following *derived reference support forces*, not automatic Pull force: Strength 5 → 222 N; 10 → 445 N; 15 → 890 N; 20 → 1,779 N; 23 → 2,669 N. Throwing velocities can provide dynamic-force evidence only when acceleration length/time is independently specified. No all-purpose numerical force law is approved.
- **Revision 55–56 throwing evidence reconciled:** Revision 55 confirmed the Throwing Table's 50-lb maximum weight at Strength 10 as the **absolute throwability** limit, not an effective adjacent-hex throw at the minimum 5 yd/s. The corresponding ~0.9 yd/s launch therefore is not contradictory. Revision 56 confirms progressive skills start at acquired **level 1**, and that the table assumes the Throw-specific +1 maximum-velocity modifier for **any acquired Throw level**. The formula gives `22.5 yd/s` at Strength 10 with Throw level 1+ and `20 yd/s` at level 0. This resolves the prior table/formula baseline ambiguity, without changing physical force calibration assumptions.
- **Revision 53 adopted physical force unit (author):** Represent applied force, including Pull and Concerted Pull resultant vectors, in **newtons (N)** (`1 N = 1 kg·m/s²`). The earlier unresolved choice between physical units and game force units is **resolved in favor of newtons**. **Still open:** mapping Strength/body attributes to a force in N and net force to acceleration, displacement, and legal positions. No dice-to-newton formula is authorized.
- **Acceptance tests r53:** (1) Equal and opposite stipulated `100 N` successes cancel to `0 N`, regardless of roll margins. (2) Orthogonal stipulated `100 N` forces produce resultant `(100 N, 100 N)`, with distance still underdetermined. (3) An uncalibrated Strength score cannot be converted to N merely by selecting the unit. (4) Concerted Pull's combined check result is not a newton magnitude.
- **Revision 52 adopted success/force distinction (author):** Contested Strength dice decide **whether a Pull succeeds**, **not how much physical force** it produces. Applied force for a successful Pull comes **primarily from the Being’s Strength and physical characteristics**, independent of rolled contest totals and victory margins. The same separation applies to combined successes and Concerted Pull: cooperative dice totals assess success, not total Newtons. **Still open:** explicitly define the physical characteristics, force model/calibration, and mapping from resultant force to displacement and legal hex motion. No unapproved numeric force formula or die-margin multiplier.
- **Acceptance tests r52:** (1) An otherwise identical successful Pull with low versus high winning dice totals has the same modeled applied force. (2) Two different Beings with differing physically relevant Strength/body attributes may exert different modeled forces under an adopted physical model even if dice totals match; do not invent their magnitudes now. (3) Two independently successful opposing Pulls exert equal effective force by physical model -> net zero even if their winning contest totals differ. (4) Concerted Pull sums rolled results to determine success but does not use their sum directly as a force vector magnitude. (5) Any failed Pull has no successful forced-movement contribution under ordinary rules. (6) Numeric results for unequal force, acceleration, or hex translation remain undetermined without the physical model.
- **Revision 51 adopted physical composition of simultaneous Pulls (author):** Opposed, independently successful Pulls resolving in the same second act as **simultaneous applied forces**, not mutually exclusive Grapple-position outcomes. Combine force vectors; **equal and opposite forces cancel to zero net displacement**. Do **not** invoke the same-second Grapple reroll procedure. A success may exert force while producing no movement; resolve net displacement once against all applicable grip, spatial, and movement restrictions. The Strength contest is a success mechanism, not a calibrated force value. **Open:** define a quantitative or scale-consistent force/acceleration-to-hex-displacement model, including how force magnitudes are assigned (roll total is not automatically force).
- **Acceptance tests r51:** (1) Two opposing same-second Pulls both succeed with equal effective force -> both contributions exist, net force zero and B does not move, with no rerolls. (2) Two successful Pulls with unequal opposed forces -> displacement, if any, follows the resultant and legal space/movement constraints, not arbitrary action evaluation order. (3) Two oblique simultaneous successful Pulls -> direction is the vector resultant, not either pull alone. (4) One Pull fails -> it has no successful forced-displacement contribution under the standard success procedure. (5) A simultaneous incompatible Pin/Escape conflict still uses the previously adopted no-time Grapple rerolls; never import that rule for physical Pulls. (6) A coordinated Concerted Pull retains its previously adopted cooperative summed-contest check, not confused with independently opposing Pulls. (7) No numeric force, acceleration, or distance is deduced from a 3d6 contest total absent an adopted physical mapping.
- **Revision 50 adopted Pull direction and Concerted Pull (author):** Revise Pull from backward-only to **non-forward** (backward, lateral, and other legal non-forward directions). **No other special Bull rush provisions**—shield, Charge, Mounted Rush, follow-up Trip/Overrun—apply. **Concerted Pull is allowed**, following Concerted Rush's prior-communication and sum-of-modified-Strength-contest-results procedure; each participant must maintain their **own** effective Grab/Hold on the common target throughout. All displacement must remain jointly legal for participating Beings. Grip release ends the relevant ongoing Pull with ordinary simultaneous-action timing. This supersedes the backward-only and undecided-special-case language of revision 49.
- **Acceptance tests r50:** (1) Forward forced movement through a grip uses Bull rush; legal lateral/backward displacement uses Pull. (2) Lateral Pull adheres to the actor's legal movement rate, physical grip and unobstructed/unoccupied geometry; no new speed. (3) Shield, Charge, Mounted Rush and follow-up Trip/Overrun bonuses or actions do not transfer to Pull. (4) Two qualified pullers who communicated intent beforehand and each hold the target roll 3d6 + Strength bonus with normal adjustments; add the modified totals on each side to decide success, no extra coordination bonus. (5) A helper without a current Grab/Hold cannot participate. (6) Coordinated displacement must be spatially feasible for **all** participating holders. (7) Releasing any participating Grab/Hold ends continuing Concerted Pull when the release commits; same-second resolved movement is not retroactively undone. (8) Grapple control remains independent of physical Pull.
- **Revision 49 adopted Hold movement and Pull (author):** A physical Grab/Hold constrains *relative movement*, not all ordinary movement. To pass beyond its limits the held Being must first Break hold. For forced movement through an existing grip, use Bull rush forward or Pull backward. Pull is the one-second backward counterpart to Bull rush, using the ordinary Strength contests, relevant size/stability and continued-success modifiers, effective movement limits and no occupied-space displacement. It requires Grab/Hold throughout; **if a Grab/Hold breaks, Pull ends** under same-second state commitment. Distinguish drawing a target toward a stationary puller from backing up while dragging a target. **Do not automatically import** shield, Charge, Concerted/Mounted Rush or follow-up Trip/Overrun benefits to Pull.
- **Acceptance tests r49:** (1) Ordinary relative movement within intact Hold limits is allowed; movement beyond them without Break hold is not. (2) Forward forced movement via grip uses Bull rush, backward via Pull. (3) Pull with no Grab/Hold is invalid. (4) Against resistance both sides use Bull rush Strength contest; ties do not move target. (5) Stationary Pull moves only the target to a legally closer unoccupied hex; moving backward allows puller and target to translate backward if both paths remain legal, with backward speed restricted by the movement rule. (6) Obstructions/occupied spaces limit or prevent displacement. (7) A released grip ends Pull with earlier- versus same-second timing respected; established Grapple is unaffected. (8) Bull rush special-case benefits not automatically granted to Pull.
- **Revision 48 adopted Escape/Hold interaction (author):** A successful Escape from an established Grapple does **not** release a separate continuing physical Hold. Any restrictions created by that Hold, including on moving to an adjacent unoccupied hex after Grapple Escape, remain effective until the physical grip separately ends. Grapple Escape resolves under its normal opposed check and position-based consequences; the adjacent move is subject to the surviving physical Hold and cannot bypass it. This requires no new check, no automatic Break hold, and no presumption that Grapple Escape itself fails when Hold prevents movement.
- **Acceptance tests r48:** (1) B succeeds at escaping A’s established Grapple while A separately Holds B: Grapple Escape applies, A’s Hold continues, and its movement restrictions remain. (2) If the existing Hold prevents the adjacent move listed for simple Grapple Escape, B cannot make that move by virtue of Escape alone. (3) If no other restriction exists, the ordinary Grapple Escape movement remains applicable. (4) A separate successful Break hold, voluntary release, or failed Grab maintenance can subsequently release the physical Hold under its own rules. (5) A failed or tied Grapple Escape has the previously specified Grapple-position result and does not alter the separate physical Hold.
- **Revision 47 adopted separation (author):** **Use Break hold to break an opponent’s physical Grab/Hold; Escape is exclusive to an established Grapple.** A Grab/Hold does not qualify for Escape, and Escape Artist does not add to the one-second Break hold opposed Strength contest. Its fixed-DC uses with ropes, entanglements, and confined spaces remain unchanged. The earlier r32 Grab-or-Grapple Escape suggestion is superseded. Physical grip loss through Break hold, failed maintenance, or voluntary release does not itself terminate an established Grapple (r46).
- **Acceptance tests r47:** (1) Only a Grab/Hold exists: Escape declaration is invalid; Break hold targets one grip using 3d6 + Strength bonus each side, one second. (2) Breaking a grip does not terminate a separate established Grapple. (3) An established Grapple permits Escape using one opposed Grapple check with Escape Artist additive to Improved Grapple. (4) No Escape Artist bonus applies to Break hold. (5) Rope/net/entanglement fixed-DC Escape Artist checks retain their own rules. (6) Earlier Grab-only Escape eligibility notes are superseded, not silently retained.
- **Revision 46 superseding clarification (author):** **Grab** is a physical grip; **Hold** continues Grab; neither is Grapple. A successful Grab is a prerequisite to initiate Grapple, but an **established** opponent’s Grapple is not terminated by breaking even the last Hold: only **Escape or unconsciousness** can terminate that opponent’s Grapple. Own Grapple action can end through action change as in r44. The r45 last-grip automatic interruption rule is **withdrawn for an established Grapple** (it applies only to an initiation not yet successfully established). A separate **Grab check** has the same 3d6 + Improved Grapple skill + best Strength/Dexterity bonus + applicable modifiers framework as the separately named **Grapple check**, but the Grab check establishes/continues a particular grip and does not resolve a Grapple action. This **supersedes r40** for unwilling-Being Grab d20-vs-d20 checks, while retaining touch, special willing/object procedures, one-second Break hold Strength contests, existing timing, and r41 coincident-first-Grapple substitution. Grapple control positions are not physical Hold states.
- **Acceptance tests r46:** (1) A Grab on B succeeds via opposed Grab check -> individual physical grip; no Grapple created. (2) Six seconds later, holder wins Grab check -> Hold continues, loser/tie -> grip ends, with no automatic effect on an **established** Grapple. (3) A breaks B’s last Hold while B is **already grappling** -> B’s pending Grapple continues normally; no forced new action. (4) A breaks B’s only Grab **before B’s first successful Grapple check** -> unestablished initiation loses prerequisite, B may choose new action. (5) B successfully Escapes A’s Grapple -> Grapple ends as specified by its position-dependent Escape rule. (6) B becomes unconscious -> B’s Grapple terminates. (7) A ends **own** Grapple by switching actions without Multi-action -> A ends, B continues. (8) First Grapple check coincident with Hold maintenance -> one Grapple check, failed first Grapple does not itself release the preceding Grab. (9) Ordinary initial touch, object Grab, and Break hold retain their separate rolls.

Apply this equally to the continuing Hold check. This does not create duplicate checks at the later participant's original timing. The six-second default and previously adopted opposed-check formula remain unchanged.
- **Adopted in r31:** When a held participant declares Escape at the next opposed Grapple skill check (at the default interval or accelerated by either participant), **that one check simultaneously tests continuation of the Hold and resolves the declared Escape**. No prerequisite hold-breaking check and no second Escape roll occur. If Escape wins, use its existing position-dependent consequences (breaking a simple Hold ends grappling, whereas escaping a Takedown or Pin may leave the combatants held); failure or tie preserves the position. The opposed formula, timing, and other action prerequisites remain unchanged.
- **Adopted in r32:** In a declared Escape check, the escaping participant adds **Escape Artist skill level and Improved Grapple skill level together** to the same opposed `3d6 + best Strength/Dexterity bonus + stated modifiers` total. The resisting participant does not add Escape Artist merely for resisting. Only one opposed roll, one applicable ability bonus, and one applicable armor penalty per participant; the already adopted timing and position-specific Escape consequences remain. The standalone fixed-DC Escape Artist procedures are unaffected.
- **Adopted in r33:** A declared **Takedown, Reverse, or Pin** uses **one opposed Grapple check** at the next legal shared interval, resolving the action and continuation of the present position simultaneously; no preliminary Hold-maintenance check or second action check. The current position’s prerequisites, size limits, outcomes, and tie-preserves-status-quo rule remain unchanged. The declaration does not create another timing event.
- **Adopted in r34:** For multiple attackers acting on the same target, **each participant makes a separate check** appropriate to the declared Grab, Hold, or Grapple action. Each qualified attacker applies the already-established **+2 × (sum of effective sizes of other attackers already holding/grappling the target) / target effective size** situational bonus, subject to the existing four-times-effective-size limit. Do not pool the checks, count an actor as one of its *other* helpers, or grant bonus eligibility from simultaneous new holds prior to their shared end-of-second commitment.
- **Adopted in r35:** After a successful Grab establishes its prerequisite, the first opposed Grapple skill check requires **six seconds of preparation by default**, subject to the same legal Combat Strategy timing reductions as later opposed Grapple checks. The successful Grab does not automatically perform or immediately resolve this first Grapple check. Grab retains its own attack/defense dice and timing; after the Grab commits, an ensuing Grapple check begins its preparation. Existing earliest-legal-shared-check timing applies.
- **Adopted in r36:** When **two** separately declared Grapple actions scheduled in the same second both succeed but the successes would establish incompatible positions or control states, **reroll both relevant action checks** using the original common pre-action state and the unchanged legal modifiers. Repeat paired checks until **one succeeds and the other fails**. If both succeed or both fail on a reroll, repeat. All rerolls occur within the **same resolution second**, without new preparation or passage of game time. Commit only the sole compatible action success, alongside unrelated compatible same-second effects. This is conflict adjudication, not a first-strike precedence or a cancellation of simultaneous attacks; the ordinary rule that a tied **single** opposed check preserves the existing position is unchanged.
- **Acceptance tests r36:** A Pin and B Escape each independently succeed in second 6 with incompatible positions: reroll both; second pair both fail: reroll both again; third pair only B succeeds: B's position-specific Escape outcome commits in second 6, and A's attempted Pin does not commit. If just one succeeds on the initial checks, use that outcome without rerolling. Two compatible independent successes commit together. No game time passes, no changed modifiers or intermediate positions are used, and no unrelated same-second attack is rerolled.
- **Adopted in r37:** Extend the r36 no-time conflict adjudication to **three or more simultaneous participants**. Reroll **every participant whose individually successful declared action is involved in a mutually incompatible set of final Grapple positions/control states**. Continue rerolling the whole involved group, using the same original pre-action snapshot and unmodified declarations/qualifications/modifiers, until **at least one** participant succeeds and **all the successful consequences are mutually compatible**. A reroll with no winners or with an incompatible combination is repeated. **More than one** winner is allowed when their outcomes are jointly compatible. No game time or additional preparation is consumed; commit all compatible winners, along with unaffected same-second results. The two-person r36 rule remains the special case in which exactly one of the two must win; the ordinary single opposed-check tie remains unchanged.
- **Acceptance tests r37:** (1) A, B and C independently succeed on declarations that cannot all coexist: reroll all three. (2) If a reroll yields two successes whose outcomes remain mutually incompatible, reroll all three again, **including** the participant who failed. (3) If the next reroll has no successes, reroll the whole group again. (4) If the next reroll yields only B successful, commit only B's action in the original resolution second. (5) If A and C succeed and their outcomes can coexist while B fails, commit A and C together, without further rerolls. (6) Freeze original modifiers and helper eligibility throughout; an unrelated simultaneous strike retains its original success/damage. (7) Two-person outcomes of two successes or two failures still reroll, until precisely one succeeds. (8) A tied single opposed check still preserves position without a fresh conflict reroll.
- **Adopted in r38:** A Being attempting to break an individual Grab **changes its action**. If its previous action has not yet resolved, it must be **canceled** and does not later resolve. An already resolved action is unaffected. Breaking one individual hold remains a deliberate **one-second action**, declared through the normal action-decision/timeline procedure; it is not a free additional reaction and does not change the distinct six-second Grapple timing. The existing text does not completely specify the **Grab-break success check**, and this revision does not invent one.
- **Acceptance tests r38:** (1) An attack in progress, due to resolve later, is canceled when its Being instead declares breaking a Grab; only the newly declared action can subsequently resolve. (2) A completed attack is not retroactively canceled. (3) One break attempt may target one hold, not all hands or grapplers simultaneously. (4) No additional zero-time break is awarded while another action continues. (5) Grapple Escape retains its approved check and timing; do not collapse it into the one-second Grab-break action. (6) Success dice and multi-hand/grip interactions remain open rather than invented.
- **Reconciled in r39 (existing source rule, no new dice mechanic):** The source [Break hold](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#break-hold) action on page 51 already defines the resolution of breaking one individual Grab: a **Strength ability contest**, in which **both participants roll 3d6 + Strength bonus**, and the higher total wins. The breaker wins only with the higher total; ties preserve the hold. Breaking the hold takes **one second** and remains a change of action that cancels an unfinished action (r38). **Do not substitute** the attack/defense comparison used to establish a Grab or the six-second opposed Improved Grapple check used for Grapple Escape. The preceding r38 uncertainty is a documentation/cross-reference oversight, not a missing author-approved mechanic; the user's “Yes” to an incorrectly presented either/or question cannot identify a choice between those alternatives.
- **Acceptance tests r39:** (1) Breaker Strength bonus +2 rolls 10 on 3d6 versus holder bonus +1 rolling 9: 12 > 10, breaker releases one targeted hold. (2) Equal final totals retain the target hold. (3) Breaker loses: hold remains. (4) The one-second contest is available only through an explicitly declared change of action and cancels an unfinished action. (5) A successful break of one of multiple hands does not automatically break the others. (6) Do not inject Improved Grapple/Escape Artist levels into the standalone Break hold Strength contest. (7) A separately declared Grapple Escape retains its own approved six-second timing/check; how eligibility overlaps with an individual Grab and multiple independent holds remains open.
- **Adopted in r43:** **Break hold during Grapple is a separate action that normally interrupts Grapple.** Without the Multi-action skill, declaring the one-second Break hold interrupts the ongoing Grapple and cancels any Grapple action still pending. With Multi-action, it is possible to continue Grapple while breaking one individual grip **only if** both actions are part of a legal declared Multiple actions design. Apply all existing concurrent-action priority penalties, skill prerequisite, timing, and physical compatibility rules. Break hold still uses its original opposed Strength ability contest; no extra or free check is introduced. This explicitly qualifies the general r38–r39 action-change statement, rather than deleting the history.
- **Acceptance tests r43 (qualified by r44):** (1) A is Grappling B without Multi-action and declares Break hold against B's individual grip: **only A's** Grapple action terminates, any pending A Grapple preparation is canceled, and a one-second Break hold is scheduled; B's Grapple action continues. (2) A's Break hold fails: no grip is released, and A's Grapple action does not resume automatically. (3) A has Multi-action and legally declares compatible Grapple and Break hold components with the required allocation and priority penalty: both may proceed under Multiple actions. (4) Mere possession of Multi-action grants no free Break hold. (5) Break hold success releases only the targeted grip, not other independent grips. (6) Strength contest and one-second timing are unchanged. (7) Physical grips do not terminate merely because A changed actions; any effect of a later successful release on B's further Grapple check eligibility remains a separate question.
- **Adopted in r45:** A successful Break hold or other release that removes the **last physical grip necessary** for B's unfinished Grapple action terminates **B's pending Grapple action at the release boundary**, cancels its unfinished preparation and scheduled check, and gives B a normal new action choice immediately (without time-free action completion). A's mere change to Break hold is insufficient. A failed/tied Break hold leaves B's Grapple unchanged. If an alternative qualifying grip remains, losing one grip alone does not force this cancellation. For a Break hold and Grapple check scheduled to resolve in the **same second**, each resolves using the single common post-movement/pre-action snapshot; the release cannot retroactively cancel B's same-second check. The release may prevent B's later unfinished Grapple work after it commits.
- **Acceptance tests r45:** (1) B's Grapple resolves in second 8; A succeeds at releasing B's only required grip in second 6: B's second-8 action immediately terminates when second-6 effects commit; B may choose a new action after the release, scheduled with ordinary preparation. (2) A simply declares Break hold at second 5: B's action remains scheduled until the grip actually ends. (3) A's Break hold fails or ties: B keeps the grip and pending Grapple. (4) An independent second qualifying grip remains after the targeted grip breaks: no automatic last-grip cancellation. (5) Both Grapple and Break hold resolve in second 6: both are evaluated before the release commits and no retroactive nullification of the same-second Grapple occurs. (6) B has already completed the Grapple action in an earlier second: no retroactive cancellation.
- **Adopted in r44:** **Grapple actions are participant-specific.** A's change to Break hold without a legal concurrent Multi-action program **terminates only A's Grapple action and cancels A's unfinished Grapple preparation**. It does **not** end or cancel B's independently declared Grapple action; B's declared Grapple remains pending under its own schedule. This action change, even before the Break hold check, does **not** itself terminate any established physical Grab. Grip termination still requires an independent applicable event or rule. A later successful Break hold releases only the targeted grip. No new Grapple opposed-check or Break hold roll mechanics, action timing, or Multi-action exceptions are introduced.
- **Acceptance tests r44:** (1) A and B have independently declared Grapple actions. A without Multi-action declares Break hold. A's Grapple action and its unfinished preparation end; B's pending Grapple remains. (2) Before the one-second Break hold resolves, B's own physical Grab remains; A's action change has not released it. (3) A loses/ties the Break hold Strength contest: target grip remains; B's Grapple action was never interrupted; A's Grapple does not restart. (4) A wins: only the targeted hold ends; independently established grips remain as applicable. (5) A has a legal Multi-action program with Grapple and Break hold: A may retain both actions subject to normal timing and penalties; B's action still independent. (6) Do not automatically treat A's cessation as the termination of B's scheduled check or establish new dice for B's ongoing Grapple; adjudication of later action eligibility after an actual grip release remains open.
- **Adopted in r42:** **An unsuccessful attempt to establish a Grapple does not terminate the prerequisite successful Grab.** Failure or tie at the first opposed Grapple check leaves that Grab in place until it is actually ended by a separate applicable event or rule, such as a later unsuccessful Grab maintenance check, successful Break hold, or voluntary release. This includes a check that replaced coincident same-grip Grab maintenance under r41: its failure is not treated as a hidden Grab-maintenance failure. Continue to distinguish the separate dice and timing procedures and independent grips.
- **Acceptance tests r42:** (1) A Grabs B, attempts the first Grapple, and loses the opposed Grapple check: no Grapple forms and the original Grab persists. (2) First Grapple check ties: no Grapple forms, Grab persists. (3) First Grapple check coincides with and replaces same-grip maintenance; A loses: **do not** roll or infer failed maintenance, Grab persists. (4) Later separately due Grab maintenance fails or ties: that Grab ends under its existing rule. (5) B succeeds at a separately declared one-second Break hold: that targeted Grab ends. (6) Another independent hand/grip is unaffected. (7) No new maintenance timing, roll, release trigger or Grapple-success consequence is invented.
- **Adopted in r41:** When the separately prepared **first opposed Grapple check** and the scheduled six-second **Grab maintenance check** would resolve in the same second **for the same prerequisite grip**, the **Grapple check replaces the maintenance check**. Do not make both rolls; the initial Grab remains a separate prerequisite, the first Grapple check retains its normal six-second base preparation (legally reducible), and other independently held grips are unaffected. This decision does not add new outcomes for a failed first Grapple check.
- **Acceptance tests r41:** (1) A establishes Grab on B, and both the first opposed Grapple check and the same-grip Grab maintenance become due in second 6: perform one opposed Grapple check, no extra d20 attack/defense maintenance. (2) With a legal shortened Grapple preparation, resolve that first Grapple check at the shortened time using the same formula, not an immediate check on Grab success. (3) An independent second hand maintaining a separate Grab is not suppressed by the first hand’s transition. (4) Grapple failure/tie uses existing Grapple consequences without inventing a new automatic release rule; track any unresolved Grab effect separately. (5) An unrelated one-second Break hold declaration remains a distinct Strength contest and action change. (6) Later ongoing Grapple checks follow the adopted Grapple maintenance rule; do not substitute Grab dice.
- **Adopted in r40:** To maintain an individual Grab on an unwilling target, repeat the **same opposed attack/defense Grab check** used when establishing the Grab, at its existing **six-second maintenance interval**. The holder uses an attack roll and attack level, and the target uses an attack/defense comparison with defense level, including applicable Grab modifiers and exceptions. The holder must obtain the higher total for the grip to continue; a failure or tie releases the particular grip. This is not the one-second Break hold Strength contest, nor is it the opposed Improved Grapple skill check used to maintain/transition an established Grapple. No new extra or zero-time check is created.
- **Acceptance tests r40:** (1) On a six-second maintenance check, holder d20=13 with attack level +2 against opponent d20=11 with defense level +1: 15>12, existing individual grip persists. (2) Holder 8+2 versus defender 12+0: grip ends. (3) Holder and defender modified totals both 15: the Grab is not maintained. (4) Breaking the grip via deliberate one-second Break hold still uses 3d6 + Strength bonuses instead, with the breaker required to win. (5) Escaping an established Grapple retains its different opposed skill-check formula and legal scheduling. (6) Grabbing a second time does not pool check rolls; independent grips remain distinguishable. (7) As of r40 this overlap was unresolved; revision 41 establishes that a coincident initial Grapple check replaces maintenance for the same grip.
- **Revision 57 adopted geometry simplification:** Reference arm length for throwing-force calibration is determined by **Size Category** using the existing Size Categories Table, rather than individually specified for every Being. **Revision 58 assigns Medium (category 3) a 0.7865 m reference length** (adult male ANSUR II mean, shoulder tip to middle fingertip); the other seven category reference lengths remain unspecified. ### ES-042 — Stun threat/critical/save decision procedure
- **Status:** **Resolved — adopted in revision 26** · **Priority:** High · **Source:** [Stun, pp. 49 and 58](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#stun); [Threat, critical hit, fatal hit, p. 12](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#threat-critical-hit-fatal-hit).
- **Adopted:** A normal attack roll is used for Stun. A threat triggers a Fortitude DC 20 save; a failed save stuns whether or not the second attack roll confirms a critical. A successful Stun + confirmed critical deals normal critical damage; a successful Stun + unconfirmed threat deals normal damage. A non-threat hit or other failed Stun on a successful hit deals half normal damage rounded down, including save success, attack DC above 20 or target immunity. A miss deals no damage. Two natural 20s still invoke the general fatal-hit rule. Stun duration is Stun skill level × d6 seconds (roll that many dice). No Stun may cancel an attack resolving in the same second; later unfinished actions are interrupted.
- **Acceptance tests:** Threat and confirmation separately permuted with Fortitude save; check damage and Stun duration, including immunity, DC >20, natural double 20, same-second attack outcomes, and pending actions. The worked example’s numerical critical-damage omission has been completed in ES-052 (revision 27).

### ES-043 — End Run size bonus and contested test

### ES-044 — Trip's abort-other-attack phrase and simultaneity
- **Status:** **Resolved — adopted in revision 29** · **Priority:** Medium · **Source:** [Trip, p. 59](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#trip); [One-second action and defense resolution](encounters-working-draft-adopted-r60.md#one-second-action-and-defense-resolution).
- **Adopted:** Successful Trip is subject to the universal simultaneous-resolution rule. An opponent's attack **resolving in the same second** still resolves normally, against the shared post-movement, pre-action state. When Trip's prone outcome is committed, an **already-in-progress attack scheduled for a later second is nullified**. Use **nullified** instead of **aborted** for this consequence, distinguishing forced non-resolution from a Being voluntarily aborting an action. Already-resolved attacks are unaffected; a Being's subsequent newly declared actions are governed by the ordinary rules for the prone condition, not automatically forbidden.
- **Acceptance tests:** Attack and Trip both resolve in second 5: both resolve; Trip's prone condition commits after the batch. Trip succeeds in second 5 against an in-progress attack scheduled for second 6: the second-6 attack is nullified. An attack completed in second 4 is unaffected by a Trip in second 5. A failed Trip does not nullify an attack. There is no processing-order dependence among actions resolving in the same second.
