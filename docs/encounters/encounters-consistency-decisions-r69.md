# Revision 69 — Draw, Ready, and Delay

**Adopted:** 13 November 2026, by explicit user decision. **Applies to:** revision 61 as supplemented by revisions 62–68. This supplement adopts the supplied Draw, Ready, and Delay clarifications. It supersedes the prior conditional-trigger interpretation of Ready and the related revision-66 statement that Ready itself was not adopted. The revision-66 record remains historical provenance; it does not control this newly adopted rule.

## Delay

Delay is a choice to wait while selecting the appropriate defense level, then choose a subsequent Action at a later committed one-second boundary. Waiting does not itself choose or trigger a future Action. A Being may instead pause an eligible Action between committed seconds and later resume that same Action with its remaining duration. A pause cannot undo completed work or effects. Do not pause spellcasting or an Action that relies on momentum, including a strike or charge. A strike may be declared to take longer than normal, but may not be paused in mid-action. Delay does not consume an Object's completed Ready preparation.

## Draw

**Timing:** Normally 2 seconds.

Draw is an Action by which a Being takes a weapon from its explicitly represented stored or carried location and makes it available for use.

Drawing a weapon normally takes two seconds unless otherwise specified in the weapon description. A Being with Quick-draw skill and advanced skill in the weapon can draw it in essentially no time.

Draw does not itself provide the timing reduction associated with Ready. A weapon that has been drawn may subsequently be readied or used without additional preparation, in which case its normal attack timing applies. Drawing and readying are distinct Actions.

## Ready

**Timing:** As specified for the Object or preparation involved.

Ready is an Action by which a Being prepares an Object for possible use in a subsequent Action. Readying completes whatever preparation can be undertaken without actually performing the intended subsequent Action.

A Being that has readied an Object is not committed to using it, to any particular Action, to a target, or to any condition under which the Object must be used.

Once completed, the preparation remains effective until the Being undertakes another Action using that Object, other than Delay. Merely waiting or delaying does not invalidate readiness.

### Readying a weapon

The timing required to strike with a weapon is given in the Weapons Tables for each eligible attack motion. One-third of the weapon's applicable attack timing, rounded down, is used to ready or re-ready it.

If the weapon has been readied and no intervening Action has used it, other than Delay, the time required for a subsequent strike is reduced by the applicable readying time. A strike can never take less than one second.

After a strike, the previous readiness no longer applies. The weapon may be readied again before another strike.

### Readying another Object

To ready another Object is to prepare it for possible use in a later Action. Preparation may involve retrieving the Object from a container, picking it up, positioning it, or otherwise making it ready for use. The required preparation and its timing depend on the Object and circumstances and must be established by the applicable rules or adjudicated by the referee.

## Implementation boundaries

- Ready is preparation only. It creates no target, trigger, condition monitor, or commitment to a later Action.
- Weapon preparation is for the explicitly selected attack motion; no unselected motion receives a timing discount.
- A Being or Object is not assumed to have a drawable source location or a free/occupied hand unless that native fact is represented.
- The essentially-no-time Quick-draw exception does not authorize inventing a zero-duration scheduled Action.
- This supplement adopts no readiness of Events.
